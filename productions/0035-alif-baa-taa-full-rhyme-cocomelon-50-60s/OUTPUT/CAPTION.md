## Facebook

Four letters. Fifty seconds. Play it twice and watch what happens. 🎵

ا Alif · ب Baa · ت Taa · ث Saa

This is the first four letters of the Arabic alphabet, sung the way children actually
learn — one letter, one movement, one clear picture on screen.

Watch how it teaches, because it is doing more than singing:

ا Alif stands up tall — so she stands up tall, arm straight above her head. Her whole
body is the letter.

ب Baa has one dot below — so she points down, once.

ت Taa has two dots on top — two fingers up.

ث Saa has three dots on top — three fingers, counted out loud: one, two, three.

That is not decoration. A three-year-old cannot yet read a dot, but they can copy a
hand. The shape goes into the body first and the eyes catch up later — which is why
every letter appears on screen at the exact moment she says it.

Sit with them the second time and do the hands together. By the third play they will
be counting the dots before she does.

👇 Which letter did yours pick up first? We are betting on ت Taa — two fingers is the
easiest one to copy.

## Posting notes

These notes are for us, not for posting.

- Sound on is essential — it is a sung rhyme. Say so in the first comment or a
  sticker. This one genuinely does not work muted, unlike the image posts.
- Emphasis is carried by line breaks and the occasional capitalised word, never by
  markdown. Facebook, Instagram, TikTok and X all print asterisks and underscores
  literally.
- The ask is calibrated ("we're betting on Taa") so a parent can agree or correct,
  which is easier than composing an answer.
- The replay instruction is the point. Rhymes work by repetition; telling parents to
  play it twice raises watch time and is honest advice rather than a growth trick.
- It is the first FOUR letters, not the whole alphabet — frame it that way. It sets up
  the rest of the series rather than promising something the video does not deliver.
- The chorus and ending are not in this cut (they need a properly sung vocal, see
  01-NOTES.md). The video closes on Saa and the brand tag. Nothing in the caption
  promises a chorus.
- Best posted when a child is with a parent and a phone — after school or early
  evening.
- Pairs naturally with the published image posts 0027–0032 as the first video in the
  children's series.
