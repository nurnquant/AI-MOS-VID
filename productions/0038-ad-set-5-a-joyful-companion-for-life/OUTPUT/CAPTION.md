# Ad copy — "A Joyful Companion for Life"

PLAIN TEXT ONLY. No asterisks, no underscores — Meta prints them literally.

Meta shows only the first ~125 characters of primary text before "See more", so
every version below front-loads the hook into the first line.

The image already carries the promise. The copy's job is to name the fear the
image quietly answers: not "will my child read Qur'an", but "will my child love
it, or dread it".

---

## Primary text A — the fear nobody says out loud

She holds it carefully before she can read a word of it. That part matters more
than we admit.

Most parents want their child to read Qur'an. Fewer of us let ourselves ask the
harder question: will they love it, or will they count the minutes until the
lesson ends?

Because we have all met the adult who can recite beautifully and does not open
it. And the one who reads slowly, imperfectly, every single day, because someone
once made it feel like a gift instead of a test.

That difference is not talent. It is how they were taught.

One teacher. One child. No class of twenty to fall behind in, no one to be
embarrassed in front of. A qualified Al-Azhar teacher who moves at your child's
pace and stops before they are tired, not after.

Start with one free class. If it is not right for your child, you will know, and
we would rather you found out for free.

Claim your free trial class today.

## Primary text B — for the parent who cannot teach it themselves

You cannot read Arabic. You have decided that means your child's ceiling is
wherever yours is.

It is not.

Plenty of the parents who come to us learned Islam later in life, or grew up
reciting without ever being taught meaning, or simply never had the chance. They
worry they are the wrong person to start their child on this.

But you are not the teacher. You are the reason they show up.

We handle the rest: qualified Al-Azhar teachers, one to one, online, at times

Your child can go further in this than you ever did. That is not a failure. That

## Notes for whoever runs this

- **Test A against B first.** They speak to two different parents: A is for the
  parent who is already committed and afraid of doing it badly; B is for the
  parent who thinks they are not qualified to start. Those are different people
  and one will be much cheaper to reach than the other.
- **Do not add a discount.** The offer is already free. Stacking urgency on top
  of free reads as pressure and undercuts the calm the picture is selling.
- The picture carries the whole emotional load. **Do not repeat "joyful
  companion" in the primary text** — saying it twice makes it a slogan instead of
  a feeling.
- **Nothing in this copy claims a specific outcome or timeline.** No "fluent in
  six months". If the ad promises something the first class cannot support, the
  free trial becomes the moment the promise breaks.
