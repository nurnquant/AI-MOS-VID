## Facebook

"Alif stands up tall!" 🌟

Four letters. That is the whole lesson today — and your child will have them by
the end of one minute.

**ا Alif** — no dots, standing straight and tall
**ب Baa** — one little dot below
**ت Taa** — two little dots on top
**ث Saa** — three little dots on top

That is really how it starts. Not with a rule, not with a worksheet. With a
child noticing that ب has one dot underneath, and ت has two on top, and feeling
clever for spotting the difference.

Watch their hands in this one. One finger down for Baa. Two up for Taa. Three
for Saa. Children learn the alphabet with their bodies before their eyes — so
let them count on their fingers with the video. Play it twice. By the second
time they will be counting before the letters appear.

**Mā shā' Allāh** — four letters is a real beginning. 🤍

Little steps along the way. We'll learn some more another day.

## Posting notes

- **61 seconds.** Fits Reels, TikTok and Shorts. Facebook feed fine. Stories
  needs a split.
- **Sound on matters** — the rhyme is the lesson. But unlike 0022 this one
  survives muted: every letter, its dot count and its name are on screen as
  cards, so a silent autoplay still teaches.
- **The engagement ask is a genuinely easy one** ("which letter first?") with
  four possible answers. Easier to answer than an open question, which is the
  point.
- **Ask parents to replay it.** Repetition is how this format works, and replays
  are watch-time.
- **Series post.** Season 2 #7 of the Rhyme Series. Worth referencing the series
  in the first comment so the next one has an audience.
- Best posting window is after-school or early evening, when children are with a
  parent and a phone.

## Production notes

- **Only Juno pronounces "Alif" correctly.** Auditioned three voices on the full
  read: Daisy said "**Aleph**", Gracie said "**Aleaf**" — both wrong for teaching
  Arabic letters. Whisper caught it. Juno was the only usable one.
- **The Saa clip was re-rolled.** The first take showed an open hand at 1s and 4s
  and only three fingers at 7s — while the rhyme counts "one, two, three". A
  wrong count in the teaching content, so it was re-generated with the hand shape
  pinned still, then frame-checked at 1s, 3s, 5s and 7s.
- **All seven Veo clips carried a music bed** (−22 to −27 dB, against −40 to
  −46 dB for genuine room tone) despite "ABSOLUTELY NO MUSIC" in every prompt.
  All clip audio discarded; the soundtrack is the Juno read plus a marimba bed
  rendered locally from the Apple DLS bank (`scripts/social/kidsmusic.m`, new).
- **All Arabic is Pillow-rendered locally.** The models were told to put no text
  anywhere, including on the classroom walls, because AI-generated Arabic is
  garbled — the set dressing is geometric patterns and children's paintings only.
- **Watermarked** — rotating gold wordmark, corners cycling per beat, clear of the
  letter cards.
