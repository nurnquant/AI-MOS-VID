# inbox/req.md — mirror concept, mockup findings

Two cheap tests, **40 credits total**, of the one thing the whole idea depends on:
does a generated mirror hold a _different_ reality from the room it stands in.
Nothing else from the brief was bought. Balance 128.1 to 88.1.

| file              | model       | cost  | length |
| ----------------- | ----------- | ----- | ------ |
| `omni-mirror.mp4` | gemini_omni | 18 cr | 6.02 s |
| `veo-mirror.mp4`  | veo3_1      | 22 cr | 8.00 s |

## The concept works. Each model got a different half of it.

**Omni got the idea right.** By the last second the glass holds a grown man
teaching seated students while the hall stays real around it. That is the brief's
image, and it arrived first time.

**Veo got the look right and the idea half right.** Beautiful hall, better light,
higher fidelity — but inside the glass the boy simply ages into a young man alone.
No library, no students, no legacy. It rendered a portrait of the future, not a
_vocation_.

## Three defects, all fixable, none free

**1. Veo baked a film strip into the picture.** Sprocket holes, edge markings and
frame numbers "400", "420", "18 N254" burnt into the image. That is the "16mm film
texture, fine grain, halation" instruction taken literally as _a photograph of
film_ rather than a film-like image. Fix: drop the word 16mm and ask for the
qualities — soft grain, gentle halation, restrained contrast — never the format.

**2. Omni put calligraphy on the walls** despite the prohibition being pinned
three ways. This is the religious risk flagged before generating: pseudo-Arabic in
a Qur'an-teaching video is not a cosmetic blemish. It will need checking frame by
frame on every clip bought, and covering or re-rolling where it appears.

**3. Both put a book in the boy's hands with markings on the cover.** Same
problem, smaller. Better to specify a **plain undecorated cover**.

## The structural problem the brief has not solved

The brief asks for a single uninterrupted take and then describes roughly 30-40 s
of action: walk, turn the mirror, reveal, react, smile, walk on, mirror exits.
**Veo caps at 8 s and Omni at 10 s.** One take buys one beat.

Three honest ways out:

- **Keep the single take, cut the action.** Stand still, turn the mirror, hold.
  One beat, done properly. Closest to the brief's spirit, cheapest to make.
- **Drop "never cuts" and build 3-4 clips.** ~90-120 cr, and the hall, wardrobe
  and both faces must hold across every clip — the Style 8 reference-image trick
  applies.
- **Two takes only:** the reveal, then the walk to the archway. A single cut is
  not a betrayal of the idea; seven would be.

## The other unresolved question: shape

The brief asks for a **wide composition**. Everything published is 9:16. These
tests are 9:16 and the hall already feels cramped — at this ratio you keep either
the architecture or the faces, not both. Worth deciding deliberately rather than
discovering it in the edit.

## Recommendation

**Omni for the reveal, because it understood the assignment**, with the prompt
tightened: plain book cover, no calligraphy anywhere, and the future scene
described as students-and-library rather than "the same child older".

Do NOT commit to the full piece yet. One more test — about 18-30 credits — should
settle whether a re-prompted Omni can hold a clean, text-free hall. If it can, the
piece is buildable for roughly 60-90 credits as two or three clips.

Current balance is **88.1 credits**, so a four-clip build is most of what is left.
That is the number to weigh before approving.
