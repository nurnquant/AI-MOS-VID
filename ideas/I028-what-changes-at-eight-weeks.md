# I028 — What Changes at Eight Weeks

Series: F — Inside the Class
Status: proposed — nothing built, nothing spent
Audience: parents. Not children.
Format: ~30 s vertical, muted-autoplay safe
Style: not yet named — you name it, it is never guessed

## Hook

> Week one they can name four letters. Week eight is where it gets interesting.

First 1.5 s. On-screen text carries this alone, because Facebook autoplays muted
and a hook that only exists in the audio does not exist.

## The turn

> The jump is not knowledge. It is that they stop asking you for help.

## Beats

- Weeks one to three: sounds and blending.
- Weeks four to six: short words and heavy repetition. This is the boring part, and it is the part that matters.
- Weeks seven to eight: they read something nobody taught them.
- That is the week parents message us about.

## Close

> Eight weeks. Twice a week. That is the arc.

## Follow-ask

> Follow for the rest.

Follows come from a series promise, not from one good video. This line is the
mechanism — without it the Reel earns reach and no followers.

## Why this should convert

- Sets a realistic expectation, which reduces disappointment and refunds and builds long trust.
- Admitting there is a boring middle is unusual in education marketing and reads as honest because it is.

## Risks and honest limits

- Only publish a timeline you can actually deliver. If eight weeks is optimistic for the average child, say ten. An over-promise here costs more than the video earns.
