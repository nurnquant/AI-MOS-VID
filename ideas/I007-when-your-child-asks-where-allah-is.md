# I007 — When Your Child Asks Where Allah Is

Series: B — The Hard Parts
Status: proposed — nothing built, nothing spent
Audience: parents. Not children.
Format: ~30 s vertical, muted-autoplay safe
Style: not yet named — you name it, it is never guessed

## Hook

> "Where is Allah?" Your answer in the next ten seconds matters more than you think.

First 1.5 s. On-screen text carries this alone, because Facebook autoplays muted
and a hook that only exists in the audio does not exist.

## The turn

> You are allowed to say you do not know how to explain it yet. You are not allowed to panic.

## Beats

- Children ask this at four to six. It is developmental, not a warning sign.
- A nervous adult teaches that the question itself is dangerous.
- Give them something true and small, at their size.
- Then let them ask again next week.

## Close

> You are not building their aqeedah in one answer. You are building whether they bring you the next question.

## Follow-ask

> Follow for the hard ones.

Follows come from a series promise, not from one good video. This line is the
mechanism — without it the Reel earns reach and no followers.

## Why this should convert

- Extremely common, rarely answered calmly on camera.
- The reframe — keep the questions coming — is the shareable part, not the answer itself.

## Risks and honest limits

- AQEEDAH CONTENT. Wording must be checked before it is recorded.
- Keep strictly to what is uncontroversial and age-appropriate. Do not improvise theology on camera.
- Recommend a teacher on your team signs off the exact script.
